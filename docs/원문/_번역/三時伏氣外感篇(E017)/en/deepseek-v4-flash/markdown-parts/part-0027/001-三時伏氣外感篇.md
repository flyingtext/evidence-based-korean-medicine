## 三時伏氣外感篇

### Source Text

若是不正穢氣觸入，或口食生冷，套用正氣散、六和湯、五積散之類；正氣受傷，肢冷呃忒，嘔吐自利，即用錢氏益黃散，有痰用星附六君子湯、理中湯等；倘熱氣深伏，煩渴引飲，嘔逆者連香飲，黃連竹茹橘皮半夏湯；熱閉神昏，用至寶丹；寒閉，用來復丹。

### Literal Translation

If improper foul qi touches the child, or one eats raw and cold food, use Zhengqi San, Liuhe Tang, and Wuji San. If the upright qi is injured, with cold limbs, hiccup, vomiting, and spontaneous diarrhea, at once use Qian's Yihuang San; if there is phlegm, use Xingfu Liujunzi Tang and Lizhong Tang. If hot qi lies deep with vexation, thirst, and retching, use Lianxiang Yin and Huanglian Zhuru Jupi Banxia Tang; if heat blocks and clouds the spirit, use Zhibao Dan; if cold blocks, use Laifu Dan.

## 三時伏氣外感篇

### Source Text

稚年夏月食瓜果，水寒之溼，著於脾胃，令人泄瀉，其寒溼積聚，未能遽化熱氣，必用辛溫香竄之氣，古方中消瓜果之積，以丁香、肉桂，或用麝香，今七香餅治瀉，亦祖此意；其平胃散、胃苓湯亦可用。

### Literal Translation

When young children eat melons and fruits in summer, the dampness of water-cold adheres to the spleen and stomach and causes diarrhea. Since this cold-damp accumulation has not yet quickly transformed into hot qi, one must use acrid-warm, fragrant-penetrating qi. In the ancient formulas for dispersing the accumulation of melons and fruits, Dingxiang and Rougui are used, or Shexiang. The present Qixiang Bing for treating diarrhea also follows this idea. Pingwei San and Weiling Tang may also be used.
