## 辨陰證陽證。

### Source Text

曰甚哉，陰陽之證，不可不詳也，遍觀內經中所說，變化百病，其源皆由喜怒過度，飲食失節，寒溫不適，勞役所傷而然，夫元氣穀氣榮氣清氣衛氣，生發諸陽上升之氣，此六者，皆飲食入胃，穀氣上行，胃氣之異名，其實一也，既脾胃有傷，則中氣不足，中氣不足，則六腑陽氣皆絕於外，故經言：五臟之氣已絕於外者，是六腑之元氣病也，氣傷臟乃病，臟病則形乃應，是五臟六腑真氣皆不足也，惟陰火獨旺，上乘陽分，故榮衛失守，諸病生焉，其中變化，皆由中氣不足，乃能生發諸疾，有脾胃以受勞役之疾，飲食又復失節，耽病日久，事息心安，飽食太甚，病乃大作，概其外傷風寒六淫客邪，皆有餘之病，當瀉不當補，飲食失節，中氣不足之病，當補不當瀉，舉世醫者，皆以飲食失節，勞役所傷，中氣不足當補之證，認作外感風寒有餘客邪之病，重瀉其陽，使榮衛之氣外絕，其死只在旬日之間，所謂差之毫釐，繆以千里，可不詳辯乎？按陰陽應象論云：天之邪氣，感則害人五臟，是八益之邪，乃風邪傷人筋骨，風從上受之，風傷筋，寒傷骨，蓋有形質之物受病也，繫在下焦肝腎是也，肝腎者，地之氣，難經解云：肝腎之氣已絕於內，以其肝主筋，腎主骨，故風邪感則筋骨疼痛，筋骨之絕，則腎肝之本亦絕矣，乃有餘之證也，又云：水穀之寒熱感則害人六腑，是六腑之病，乃內傷飲食也，黃帝鍼經解云：適飲食不節，勞役所傷，濕從下受之，謂脾胃之氣不足而反下行，極則衝脈之火逆而上，是無形質之元氣受病也，繫在上焦心肺是也，心肺者，天之氣，故難經解云：心肺之氣已絕於外，以其心主榮，肺主衛，榮者，血也，脈者，血之府，神之所居也，衛者，元氣，七神之別名，衛護周身在於皮毛之間也，肺絕則皮毛先絕，神無所依，故內傷飲食則亦惡風寒，是榮衛失守，皮膚間無陽以滋養，不能任風寒也，毛之絕，則心肺之本亦絕矣，蓋胃氣不升，元氣不生，無滋養心肺，乃不足之證也，計受病之人，飲食失節，勞役所傷，因而飽食內傷者極多，外傷者間而有之，世俗不知，往往將元氣不足之證，便作外感風寒表實之證而反瀉心肺，是重絕其表也，安得不死乎？古人所謂：實實虛虛，醫殺之耳，若曰不然，請以眾人之耳聞目見者證之，向者壬辰改元京師，戒嚴迨三月，下旬受敵者，凡半月解圍，之後都人之不受病者，萬無一二，既病而死者，繼踵而不絕，都門十有二所，每日各門所送，多者二千，少者不下一千，似此者，幾三月，此百萬人豈俱感風寒外傷者耶？大抵人在圍城中，飲食不節，乃勞役所傷，不待言而知，由其朝饑暮飽，起居不時，寒溫失所，動經三兩月，胃氣虧乏久矣，一旦飽食大過，感而傷人，而又調治失宜，其死也無疑矣，非惟大梁為然，遠在真祐興定間，如東平如太原如鳳翔，解圍之後，病傷而死，無不然者，余在大梁，凡所親見，有表發者，有以巴豆推之者，有以承氣湯下之者，俄而變結胸發黃，又以陷胸湯丸及茵陳湯下之，無不死者，蓋初非傷寒，以調治差誤變而似真傷寒之證，皆藥之罪也，往者不可追，來者猶可及，輒以平生已試之效著，內外傷辯論一篇，推明前哲之餘，論歷舉近世之變故，庶幾同志者審其或中觸類而長之，免後人之橫夭耳，僭易之罪，將何所逃乎？

### Literal Translation

[The author] said: How important! The patterns of yin and yang must be examined in detail. Surveying everything said in the Suwen, the changes of the hundred diseases all originate in the excesses of joy and anger, loss of regulation in diet, discomfort in cold and warmth, and injury from toil. Now, the Original Qi, the Grain Qi, the Nutritive Qi, the Clear Qi, the Defensive Qi, and the qi that engenders the yang and makes it ascend — these six are all, when food and drink enter the stomach, the ascending of grain qi — different names of the stomach qi, which in reality is one and the same. Since the spleen and stomach are damaged, the middle qi is insufficient; when the middle qi is insufficient, then the yang qi of the six bowels is all severed externally. Hence the Classic states: "Where the qi of the five viscera has already been severed externally, this is disease of the original qi of the six bowels." When qi is wounded, the viscera then become diseased; when the viscera are diseased, then the form responds. This is all insufficiency of the true qi of the five viscera and six bowels. Only the yin fire alone blazes, riding above into the yang portion, so the Nutritive and Defensive [qi] lose their guard, and all the diseases arise. Among their changes, all arise from insufficiency of the middle qi, and only then is it able to engender all the diseases. With the spleen and stomach receiving the ailment of toil, and food and drink again losing their regulation, lingering in illness for a long time, and when affairs are put aside and the mind is at ease and one eats to great repletion, the disease then greatly breaks out. Generally speaking, external cold-damage [from] wind-cold and the six excesses as guest pathogens are all patterns of excess; they should be drained and should not be supplemented. [For] disease of insufficiency of the middle qi from loss of regulation in food and drink, one should supplement and should not drain. The physicians of all the world all take the pattern that should be supplemented — insufficiency of the middle qi from loss of regulation in food and drink and injury from toil — and regard it as the disease of external contraction of wind-cold with excess guest pathogen, and heavily drain its yang, causing the qi of the Nutritive and Defensive [qi] to be severed externally; their death occurs only within a matter of days. This is what is called "the error of a hair's breadth [leads to] error by a thousand li." How can it fail to be carefully differentiated? According to the Yin-Yang Ying-Xiang Lun: "The pathogenic qi of Heaven, when contracted, harms the five viscera of man." This is the eight-benefit pathogen; it is the wind pathogen that wounds the sinews and bones of man. Wind is received from above; wind wounds the sinews, cold wounds the bones. This is disease affecting things that have form and substance, tied to the kidney and liver of the lower burner. The kidney and liver are the qi of Earth. The Nanjing commentary explains: "The qi of the liver and kidney is already severed within." Because the liver governs the sinews and the kidney governs the bones, when the wind pathogen is contracted, there is pain in the sinews and bones. When the sinews and bones are severed, then the root of the kidney and liver is also severed. This is a pattern of excess. It is also said: "The cold and heat of water and grain, when contracted, harm the six bowels of man." This is disease of the six bowels; it is internal injury of food and drink. The Huangdi Zhenjing commentary explains: "Inappropriate and immoderate food and drink, and injury from toil, dampness is received from below." It means that the qi of the spleen and stomach being insufficient instead descends; and when it reaches its extreme, the fire of the Chong vessel rebels and ascends. This is disease of the Original Qi that has no form and substance, tied to the heart and lung of the upper burner. The heart and lung are the qi of Heaven. Hence the Nanjing commentary explains: "The qi of the heart and lung is already severed externally." Because the heart governs the Nutritive [qi] and the lung governs the Defensive [qi]. The Nutritive [qi] is the blood; the vessels are the abode of the blood, where the spirit resides. The Defensive [qi] is the Original Qi, another name for the seven spirits, guarding and protecting the entire body and being between the skin and body hair. When the lung is severed, then the body hair is first severed, and the spirit has nothing on which to rely. Hence, in internal injury of food and drink, one likewise hates wind and cold, because the Nutritive and Defensive [qi] have lost their guard and there is no yang between the skin to nourish it, so it cannot withstand wind and cold. When the hair is severed, then the root of the heart and lung is also severed. Because the stomach qi does not ascend, the Original Qi is not engendered, and there is no nourishment for the heart and lung — this is a pattern of insufficiency. Reckoning the patients who have contracted disease — those who, with loss of regulation in food and drink and injury from toil, and having for that reason eaten to repletion, internally injure — are extremely many; those with external injury occur only occasionally. The common people do not know this, and often take the pattern of insufficiency of the Original Qi and, treating it as a pattern of external contraction of wind-cold with excess in the surface, instead drain the heart and lung, which is heavily severing its exterior. How can [the patient] not die? This is what the ancients called "[making] the excess more excess and the vacuity more vacuous — the physician kills them." If one says it is not so, may I attest to it with what the ears have heard and eyes have seen of all people? In former times, in the year Ren-chen when [the reign name] was changed, the capital was under martial law for about three fourt... (three months?) — actually about three months until the latter part; those who were attacked numbered half a month. After the siege was lifted, among the people of the capital those who did not contract disease were hardly one in ten thousand. And those who, having contracted disease, died followed on one another without interruption. The capital's gates numbered twelve; daily from each gate those sent out [the dead] — at most two thousand, at least not below one thousand. Like this for nearly three months. Were these million people all infected by wind-cold as external injury? By and large, people in a besieged city, with losses in the regulation of food and drink and injury from toil, need no words to know it. Because of their morning hunger and evening repletion, irregular living and resting, and loss of regulation in cold and warmth, by toil passing three or two months, the stomach qi had long become deficient and depleted. Once [a person] eats to great repletion and excess, and being affected [by it] injures himself, and moreover the regulation and treatment are not appropriate, his death is beyond doubt. It is not only Daliang that was like this; far back in the periods Zhenyou and Xingding, whether in Dongping, in Taiyuan, or in Fengxiang, after [various] sieges were lifted, those who fell ill with injury and died were none of them otherwise. When I was in Daliang, among all that I personally saw, there were some [treated] by sweating from the exterior, some by pushing [the bowels] with Ba Dou (croton), some by draining with Cheng Qi Tang. Soon [these] changed into bound-chest [pattern] and jaundice; they then drained with Xianxiong Tang and pills and with Yin Chen Tang — none who did this failed to die. For it was at the outset not cold damage; because of error in regulation and treatment it changed and became like a true cold-damage pattern. All were the fault of medicine. What is past cannot be pursued, but what is to come can still be reached. Thereupon I have taken the effective results already tested in my life and written this discourse "Differentiation of Internal and External Injury," carrying forward the remainder of the former sages, discussing and enumerating the changes and events of recent generations, so that perhaps those of like mind may examine wherein it hits, and by analogy extend it, sparing later men from untimely death. As to the offense of my presumption [in writing], from where can I escape?
