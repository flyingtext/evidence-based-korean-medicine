## 辯寒熱。

### Source Text

外傷寒邪之證與飲食失節勞役形質之病，及內傷飲食俱有寒熱，舉世盡將內傷飲食失節勞役不足之病，作外傷寒邪表實有餘之證，反瀉其表，枉死者，豈勝言哉？皆由不別其寒熱耳，今細為分解之，外傷寒邪，發熱惡寒，寒熱併作，其熱也，翕翕發熱，又為之拂拂，發熱發於皮毛之上，如羽毛之拂，明其熱在表也，是寒邪犯高之高者也，皮膚毛腠者，陽之分也，是衛之元氣所滋養之分也，以寒邪乘之，鬱遏陽分，陽不得伸，故發熱也，其面赤，鼻氣壅塞不通，心中煩悶，稍似袒裸露其皮膚，已不能禁其寒矣，其表上虛熱，止此而已，其惡寒也，雖重衣下幕，逼近烈火，終不能禦其寒，一時一日增加愈甚，必待傳入裏作下證乃罷，其寒熱齊作，無有間斷也，其內傷飲食不節或勞役所傷，亦有頭痛項強腰痛，與太陽表證微有相似，餘皆不同，論中辯之矣，內傷不足之病，表上無陽，不能禁風寒也，此則常常有之，其躁熱發於腎間者，間而有之，與外中寒邪，略不相似，其惡風寒也，蓋脾胃不足，榮氣下流而乘腎肝，此痿厥氣逆之漸也，若胃氣平常，飲食入胃，其榮氣上行以舒於心肺，以滋養上焦之皮膚，腠理之元氣也，既下流其心肺，無所稟受，皮膚間無陽，失其榮衛之外護，故陽分皮毛之間虛弱，但見風見寒，或居陰寒處，無日陽處，便惡之也，此常常有之，無間斷者也，但避風寒及溫煖處，或添衣蓋溫養其皮膚，所惡風寒便不見矣，是熱也，非表傷寒邪，皮毛間發熱也，乃腎間受脾胃下流之濕氣，閉塞其下，致陰火上衝，作蒸蒸而躁熱，上徹頭頂，傍徹皮毛，渾身躁熱作，須待袒衣露居，近寒涼處即已，或熱極而汗出而亦解，彼外傷惡寒發熱，豈有汗出者乎？若得汗則病愈矣，以此辨之，豈不如黑白之易見乎？當內虛而傷之者，躁熱也，或因口吸風寒之氣，鬱其陰火，使咽膈不通，其吸入之氣，欲入為膈上衝脈之火所拒，使陰氣不得入，其胸中之氣為外風寒所遏而不得伸，令人口開目瞪，極則聲發於外，氣不能上下，塞於咽中而氣欲絕，又或因噦因嘔因吐而躁熱，發必有所因，方有此證，其表虛，惡風寒之證復見矣，表虛之弱，為陰火所乘，躁發須臾而過，其表虛無陽，不任風寒，復見矣，是表虛無陽，常常有之，其躁熱則間而有之，此二者不齊，躁作寒已，寒作躁已，非如外傷之寒熱齊作，無有間斷也，百病俱有身熱，又謂之肌熱，又謂之皮膚間熱，以手捫之方知者是也，乃肌體有形之熱也，亦須皆待陰陽既和，汗出則愈矣，慎不可於此上辯之，以其虛實，內外病皆有之故難辯耳，只依此說，病人自覺發熱惡寒之熱及躁作之熱上辯之為准則矣。

辨外感八風之邪，或有飲食勞役所傷之重者，三二日間特與外傷者相似，其餘證有特異名者，若不將兩證重別分解，猶恐將內傷不足之證誤作有餘外感風邪，雖辭理有重複處，但欲病者易辯，醫者易治耳。

外感八風之邪乃有餘證也，內傷飲食不節，勞役所傷皆不足之病也，其內傷亦惡風自汗，若在溫暖無風處，則不惡寒，與外傷鼻流清涕，頭痛自汗頗相似，細分之，特異耳，外感風邪其惡風自汗，頭痛鼻流清涕常常有之，一日一時，增加愈甚，直至傳入裏，作下證乃罷，語聲重濁，高厲有力，鼻息壅塞而不通，能食腹中和，口知味，大小便如常，筋骨疼痛不能動搖，便著床枕，非扶不起，其內傷與飲食不節，勞役所傷然，亦惡風，居露地中遇大漫風起卻不惡也，惟門窗隙中些小賊風來，必大惡也，與傷風傷寒俱不同矣，況鼻流清涕，頭痛自汗，間或有之，鼻中氣短，少氣不足以息，語則氣短而怯弱，妨食或食不下，或不欲食，三者互有之，腹中不和，或腹中急而不能伸口，不知五穀之味，小便頻數而不渴，初勞役得病，食少，小便赤黃，大便常難或澀或結或虛坐，只見些小白膿，時有下氣或泄黃如糜，或溏泄色白或結而不通，若心下痞，或胸中閉塞如刀劙之痛，二者亦互作，不併出也，有時胃脘當心而痛，上支兩脇痛，必臍下相火之勢如巨川之水不可遏而上行，使陽明之經逆行亂於胸中，其氣無止息，甚則高喘，熱傷元氣，令四肢不收，無氣以動而懶倦嗜臥，以其外感風寒，俱無此證，故易為分辯耳。

### Literal Translation

The pattern of external contraction of the cold pathogen, the ailment of physical form from loss of regulation in food and drink and toil, and internal injury of food and drink all have cold and heat. All the world takes the disease of insufficiency from internal injury of food and drink, loss of regulation, and toil and treats it as the external contraction of the cold pathogen with excess and repletion in the surface, and instead drains its surface. Are the deaths from this wrongness countable? They all arise from not distinguishing its cold and heat. Now let me minutely decompose it. In external contraction of the cold pathogen, [there is] heat [with] aversion to cold, and cold and heat appear together. Its heat, [being] "翕翕" fluttering heat, is also called "拂拂" — heat that arises upon the skin and hair, like the brushing of a feather. This makes clear that its heat is in the surface; it is the cold pathogen invading the very tops of high [levels]. The skin, hair, and interstices are the yang portion, the portion nourished by the Original Qi of the defensive [qi]. Being seized by the cold pathogen, the yang portion is constricted and suppressed, the yang cannot stretch forth, and therefore [there is] fever. Its face is red, the nasal qi is obstructed and not clear, and within the heart [there is] vexation and oppression. Slightly exposing the skin of the body, [the patient] is already unable to endure its cold. Its superficial vacuous heat is only this and nothing more. As to its aversion to cold, even with heavy clothing, covered under curtains, or drawn near to a blazing fire, [the patient] can in the end not resist its cold. Moment by moment and day by day it increases and becomes more severe; only after it must transmit into the interior and form the lower [bowel] pattern does it cease. Its cold and heat appear together, without any interruption. As to internal injury of food and drink, or injury from toil, there is also headache, stiff nape, and lumbar pain, in slight similarity to the taiyang surface pattern; the rest are all different, and [I] have differentiated this in the discourse. In disease of insufficiency from internal injury, there is no yang on the surface, and [the patient] cannot withstand wind and cold; this occurs constantly. Its restless heat arising between the kidneys occurs only occasionally. This is in no way similar to externally contracted cold pathogen. Its aversion to wind and cold arises because the nutritive qi, from insufficiency of the spleen and stomach, flows downward and seizes the kidney and liver — this is the bud of wilting, reversal, and uprushing qi. If the stomach qi were normal, when food and drink enter the stomach, its nutritive qi ascends to spread over the heart and lung, to nourish the skin of the upper burner and the Original Qi of the interstices. Since it already flows downward, the heart and lung have nothing received; there is no yang between the skin; [the patient] loses the outer guard of the Nutritive and Defensive [qi]; hence the yang portion, between skin and body hair, becomes weak and vacuous. Only when [the patient] sees wind or cold, or when dwelling in a yin-cold place or in a place without sunlight, does he hate it. This is constant, without interruption. But when [the patient] avoids wind and cold and keeps to warm places, or adds clothing and covers to warm and nourish his skin, the hated wind and cold then no longer appear. As for this heat — it is not heat from surface contraction of cold pathogen arising between skin and body hair, but rather the damp qi that the kidney receives from the downward flow of the spleen and stomach, which blocks its lower part and causes the yin fire to gush upward, producing steaming restless heat that reaches up through the crown of the head, spreads sideways through the skin and hair, and generates steaming restless heat over the whole body. Only when [the patient] removes clothing and exposes himself, or approaches a cool place, does it cease; or when heat reaches its extreme and sweat issues, it also resolves. As for that external damage's aversion to cold and fever — does it ever produce sweat? If [the patient] obtains sweat then the disease is cured. Using this to differentiate, is it not as easy to see as black and white? When [a person] is injured because his interior is vacuous, [there is] restless heat. Or because wind-cold qi is drawn in through the mouth, suppressing its yin fire and causing the pharynx and diaphragm to be obstructed; the drawn-in qi, wishing to enter, is repelled by the fire of the Chong vessel above the diaphragm, causing the yin qi not to enter; the qi within the chest is suppressed by external wind-cold and cannot stretch forth, causing the mouth to open and the eyes to stare; in the extreme, the voice issues externally, and the qi cannot go up or down, blocked in the throat, and the qi is about to be severed. Or again because of hiccup, because of vomiting, the restless heat arises and must have a cause in order for this pattern to appear. Then the surface vacuity's pattern of aversion to wind and cold again appears. The weakness of the vacuous surface, being seized by the yin fire, [and] restless heat arises but passes in an instant; then the surface vacuity without yang, unable to withstand wind and cold, again appears. This surface vacuity without yang is constant; its restless heat occurs only occasionally. These two are not together: when restless, the cold has ceased; when cold, the restless has ceased — not like the cold and heat of external injury that appear together without interruption. All the hundred diseases have body heat, also called muscular heat, also called heat between skin and body. It is that which one only knows by feeling it with the hand. This is the fever of the body that has form and substance. It too must await the harmonization of yin and yang, and it resolves when sweat issues. One must be careful not to use this to differentiate, because vacuity and repletion, internal and external diseases all have it, and so it is hard to differentiate. Only following this explanation — [using] the heat that the patient himself feels as fever and aversion to cold, and the heat of the restless attacks — as the standard [in differentiation] is the criterion.
