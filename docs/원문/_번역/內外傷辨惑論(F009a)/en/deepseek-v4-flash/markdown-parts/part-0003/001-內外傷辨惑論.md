## 辯脈。

### Source Text

古人以脈上辯內外傷於人迎氣口，人迎脈大於氣口為外傷，氣口脈大於人迎為內傷，此辯固是，但其說有所未盡耳，外感風寒皆有餘之證，是從前客邪來也，其病必見於左手，左手主表，乃行陽二十五度，內傷飲食及飲食不節勞役不節，皆不足之病也，必見於右手，右手主裏，乃行陰二十五度，故外感寒邪，則獨左寸人迎脈浮緊，按之洪大緊者，急甚於弦，是足太陽寒水之脈，按之洪大而有力，中見手少陰心火之脈，丁與壬合，內顯洪大，乃傷寒脈也，若外感風邪，則人迎脈緩而大於氣口一倍或兩倍三倍，內傷飲食，則右寸氣口脈大於人迎一倍，傷之重者，過在少陰則兩倍，太陰則三倍，此內傷飲食之脈，若飲食不節勞役過甚，則心脈變見於氣口，是心火刑肺，其肝木挾心火之勢亦來薄肺，經云：侮所不勝，寡於畏者是也，故氣口脈急大而澀數，時一代而澀也，澀者，肺之本脈，代者，元氣不相接，脾胃不及之脈洪大而數者，心脈刑肺也，急者，肝木挾心火而反克肺金也，若不甚勞役，惟右關脾脈大而數，謂獨大於五脈，數中顯緩，時一代也，如飲食不節，寒溫失所，則先右關胃脈損，弱甚則隱而不見，惟內顯脾脈之大數，微緩時一代也，宿食不消則獨右關脈沉而滑，經云：脈滑者，有宿食也，以此辯之，豈不明白易見乎？但恐山野間，卒無醫者，何以診候，故復說病證以辯之。

### Literal Translation

The ancients, in the pulse, differentiated internal and external injury at the Renying and Qikou [positions]. If the Renying pulse is larger than the Qikou, it is external injury; if the Qikou pulse is larger than the Renying, it is internal injury. This differentiation is certainly correct, but its explanation has something incomplete. External contraction of wind-cold are all patterns of excess; this is the guest pathogen that comes from before [externally]. Its disease must be seen in the left hand; the left hand governs the surface and travels the yang twenty-five measures. Internal injury of food and drink, and immoderate food and drink and immoderate toil, are all diseases of insufficiency; they must be seen in the right hand; the right hand governs the interior and travels the yin twenty-five measures. Hence in external contraction of cold pathogen, then the Renying pulse of the left cun [position] alone is floating and tight; pressed, it is surging, large, and tight — urgent beyond [that of] the string [pulse]. This is the pulse of the foot-taiyang cold-water; pressed, it is surging, large, and forceful, and within, the heart-fire pulse of the hand-shaoyin is seen. Ding and Ren unite, and within there appears surging and large — this is the cold-damage pulse. If there is external contraction of the wind pathogen, then the Renying pulse is moderate and larger than the Qikou by double or two or three times. In internal injury of food and drink, then the Qikou pulse of the right cun is larger than the Renying by one-fold; in heavy injury, if the excess lies in the shaoyin, then by two-fold; if in the taiyin, then by three-fold. This is the pulse of internal injury of food and drink. If food and drink are immoderate and toil is excessive, then the heart pulse is seen transformed at the Qikou; this is heart-fire punishing the lung, and its liver-wood also, carrying the force of heart-fire, comes likewise to oppress the lung. The Classic states: "[He who] insults what does not conquer [him]... " — "insulating the one lacking fear." Hence the Qikou pulse is urgent, large, astringent, and rapid, and at times it is one-ternion with astringency. The astringent [pulse] is the root pulse of the lung; the ternion [intermittent] pulse means the Original Qi is not connecting [through], the pulse of insufficiency of the spleen and stomach; surging, large, and rapid — heart pulse punishing the lung; urgent — the liver-wood, carrying heart-fire, instead overcomes the lung-metal. If the toil is not severe, then only the right guan spleen pulse is large and rapid — called solely larger than the five pulses — and within the rapid [beat] appears moderate, at times one-ternion. If food and drink are immoderate and cold and warmth are lost in their place, then first the stomach pulse of the right guan is damaged; when weak in the extreme it becomes hidden and unseen, with only the spleen pulse's large rapidity appearing within, slightly moderate, at times one-ternion. If stagnant food is undigested, then only the right guan pulse alone is deep and slippery. The Classic states: "A slippery pulse means there is stagnant food." Using this to differentiate, is it not clear and easy to see? But I only fear that in mountain villages, where there are suddenly no physicians, how can diagnosis by pulse be carried out? Therefore I will also speak the symptoms to differentiate them.
