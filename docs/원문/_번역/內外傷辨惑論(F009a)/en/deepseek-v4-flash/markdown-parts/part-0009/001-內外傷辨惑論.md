## 辯筋骨四肢。

### Source Text

內傷等病，是心肺之氣已絕於外，必怠惰嗜臥，四肢沉困不收，此乃熱傷元氣，脾主四肢，既為熱所乘，無氣以動，經云：熱傷氣，又云：熱則骨消筋緩，此之謂也，若外傷風寒，是腎肝之氣已絕於內，腎主骨為寒，肝主筋為風，自古腎肝之病同一治，以其遞相維持也，故經言：膽主筋，膀胱主骨是也，或中風或傷寒，得病之日便著床枕，非扶不起，筋骨為之疼痛，不能動搖，乃形質之傷，經云：寒傷形，又云：寒則筋攣骨痛，此之謂也。

### Literal Translation

In diseases of internal injury and the like, the qi of the heart and lung is already severed externally; one is of necessity lethargic and loves recumbence, heavy and indolent, unable to gather the four limbs. This is heat wounding the Original Qi. The spleen governs the four limbs, and since [the four limbs] are already seized by heat, there is no qi to move. The Classic states: "Heat wounds the qi," and again: "Heat then the bones melt and the sinews slacken." This refers to this. In external contraction of wind-cold, the qi of the kidney and liver is already severed internally. The kidney governs the bones [and] cold; the liver governs the sinews [and] wind. Since ancient times the diseases of the kidney and liver have been treated together, because they sustain each other in turn. Hence the Classic says: "The gallbladder governs the sinews; the bladder governs the bones." Whether suffering wind stroke or cold damage, on the day of contracting the disease one takes to bed and pillow; unless supported, one cannot rise; the sinews and bones become painful and cannot be moved or swayed. This is injury to the concrete substance. The Classic states: "Cold wounds the form," and again: "Cold then the sinews contract and the bones ache." This refers to this.
