## 辯渴與不渴。

### Source Text

外感風寒之邪，三日已，外穀消，水去邪氣傳裏，始有渴也，內傷飲食失節，勞役久病者，必不渴，是邪氣在血脈中，有餘故也，初勞役形質，飲食失節，傷之重者，必有渴，以其心火熾，上克於肺金，故渴也，又當以此辯之，雖渴欲飲冷水者，當徐徐少與之，不可縱意而飲，恐水多峻下，則胃氣愈弱，輕則為脹，重則傳變諸疾，必反覆悶亂，百脈不安，夜加增劇，不得安臥，不可不預度也。

### Literal Translation

When the pathogen of externally contracted wind-cold [strikes], after three days the external grain has been digested, the water departed, and the pathogen qi transmitted into the interior; only then does thirst begin. In internal injury of food and drink, loss of regulation, and in long-standing disease from toil, there must be no thirst, because the pathogen qi is in the blood vessels and is in excess. At the outset of injury by toil of the physical form and loss of regulation of food and drink, when the injury is severe, there must be thirst, because its heart fire blazes and attacks downward the lung metal, and therefore [there is] thirst. Moreover, one should use this to differentiate. Although [the patient] is thirsty and wishes to drink cold water, one should give it little by little and slowly; one must not allow free indulgence in drinking, fearing that with much water descending abruptly, the stomach qi will become all the weaker; in light cases it becomes distension, in heavy cases it transmits and changes into many diseases, and it will certainly repeatedly become oppressed and in chaos, and the hundred vessels will be unrestful; at night it increases and becomes more severe, and [the patient] cannot get restful recumbence. One cannot but prefigure this.
