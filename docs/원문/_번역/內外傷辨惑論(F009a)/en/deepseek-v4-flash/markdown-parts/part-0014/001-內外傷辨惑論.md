## 飲食勞倦論。

### Source Text

古之至人，竅於陰陽之化，究乎生死之際，所著內經，悉言人以胃氣為本，蓋人受水穀之氣以生，所謂清氣榮氣衛氣春升之氣，皆胃氣之別稱也，夫胃為水穀之海，飲食入胃，遊溢精氣，上輸於脾，脾氣散精，上歸於肺，通調水道，下輸膀胱，水精四布，五經並行，合於四時五臟陰陽揆度以為常也，苟飲食失節，寒溫不適，則脾胃乃傷，喜怒憂恐勞役過度而損耗元氣，既脾胃虛衰，元氣不足，而心火獨盛，心火者，陰火也，起於下焦，其系繫於心，心不主令，相火代之，相火，下焦包絡之火，元氣之賊也，火與元氣不能兩立，一勝則一負，脾胃氣虛則下流於腎肝，陰火得以乘其土位，故脾胃之證，始符之則氣高而喘，身熱而煩，其脈洪大而頭痛，或渴不止，皮膚不任風寒而生寒熱，蓋陰火上衝，則氣高而喘，身煩熱為頭痛為渴而脈洪大，脾胃之氣下流，使穀氣不得升浮，是生長之令不行，則無陽以護其榮衛，不任風寒乃生寒熱，皆脾胃之氣不足所致也，然而與外感風寒所得之證頗同而理異，內傷脾胃乃傷其氣，外感風寒乃傷其形，傷外為有餘，有餘者瀉之，傷內為不足，不足者補之，汗之下之吐之克之皆瀉也，溫之和之調之養之皆補也，內傷不足之病，苟誤認作外感有餘之病而反瀉之，則虛其虛也，難經云：實實虛虛，損不足而益有餘，如此死者，醫殺之耳，然則奈何？曰：惟當以甘溫之劑，補其中升其陽，甘寒以瀉其火則愈，內經曰：勞者溫之，損者溫之，蓋溫能除大熱，大忌苦寒之藥瀉胃土耳，令立補中益氣湯。

補中益氣湯：

黃耆（勞役病熱甚者一錢），甘草（炙各五分），人參（去蘆），升麻，柴胡，橘皮，當歸身（酒洗），白朮（各三分）。

右件㕮咀，都作一服，水二盞煎至一盞，去柤，早飯後溫服，如傷之重者二服而愈，量輕重治之。

### Literal Translation

The perfected persons of antiquity, having penetrated the transformations of yin and yang and investigated the boundary of life and death, compiled the Suwen, which entirely says that man takes the stomach qi as his root. Because man comes to life by receiving the qi of water and grain — what is called the clear qi, the nutritive qi, the defensive qi, and the spring-ascending qi are all alternate names of the stomach qi. Now the stomach is the sea of water and grain. When food and drink enter the stomach, the essential qi roams and overflows, ascending to be transmitted to the spleen; the spleen qi disperses the essence, which ascends to return to the lung; it regulates and frees the waterways, descending to be transported to the bladder; the essence of water is distributed in four directions; the five channels proceed in parallel; united with the four seasons and the yin-yang ceasure of the five viscera, this is held to be the norm. If food and drink lose their regulation and cold and warmth are uncomfortable, then the spleen and stomach are thereby injured; and excess of joy, anger, anxiety, fear, and toil depletes the Original Qi. When the spleen and stomach are already vacuous and declining, and the Original Qi is insufficient, the heart fire alone is over-prosperous. The heart fire is the yin fire; it arises from the lower burner, and its connection ties to the heart. The heart does not preside over orders; the minister-fire acts in its place. The minister-fire, the fire of the pericardium [channel] of the lower burner, is the bandit of the Original Qi. Fire and Original Qi cannot both stand; if one conquers, the other loses. When the spleen-stomach qi is vacuous, it flows downward to the kidney and liver, and the yin fire is thereby able to seize its earth position. Hence in the patterns of the spleen and stomach, at the beginning: the qi being high, [there is] wheezing; the body hot and vexed; its pulse surging and large, with headache; or unceasing thirst; the skin not able to withstand wind and cold, and giving rise to cold and heat. Because the yin fire gushes upward, then the qi is high and [there is] wheezing; with the body hot, vexed, [there is] headache, [there is] thirst, and the pulse is surging and large. The spleen-stomach qi flowing downward causes the grain qi not to ascend and float, so the order of growth and engendering is not carried out; then there is no yang to guard its Nutritive and Defensive [qi], and not being able to withstand wind and cold, cold and heat arise. These all arise from insufficiency of the spleen-stomach qi. Nevertheless, this is quite similar to the pattern obtained from external contraction of wind-cold, but the principle is different. Internal injury of the spleen and stomach wounds its qi; external contraction of wind-cold wounds its form. Wounding the external is excess; excess should be drained. Wounding the internal is insufficiency; insufficiency should be supplemented. Sweating, draining downward, vomiting, and restraining are all draining; warming, harmonizing, regulating, and nourishing are all supplementing. In the disease of insufficiency from internal injury, if one mistakenly recognizes it as a disease of excess from external contraction and instead drains it, then one makes the vacuous more vacuous. The Nanjing states: "Making the excess more excess and the vacuous more vacuous, depleting the insufficient and augmenting the excess — those who die thus are killed by the physician." If that is so, what then? [The answer] says: One should only use sweet-warm medicinals to supplement its center and raise its yang, and sweet-cold to drain its fire; then it [is] cured. The Suwen states: "In toil, warm it; in debilitation, warm it." Because warmth can eliminate great heat. One greatly forbids bitter-cold medicinals to drain the stomach earth. Thus I establish the Buying Zhongqi Tang (Tonify-the-Center Augment-Qi Decoction).
