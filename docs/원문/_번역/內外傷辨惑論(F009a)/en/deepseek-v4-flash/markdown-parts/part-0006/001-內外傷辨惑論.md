## 辯口鼻。

### Source Text

若飲食勞役所傷，其外證必顯在口，必口失穀味，必腹中不和，必不欲言，縱勉強對答，聲必怯弱，口沃沬多唾，鼻中清涕或有或無，即陰證也，外傷風寒，則其外證必顯在鼻，鼻氣不利，聲重濁不清利，其言壅塞，盛有力而口中必和，傷寒則面赤，鼻壅塞而乾，傷風則鼻流清涕而已，內經云：鼻者，肺之候，肺氣通於天，外傷風寒則鼻為之不利，口者，坤土也，脾氣通於口，飲食失節，勞役所傷，口不知穀味，亦不知五味，又云：傷食惡食，傷食明矣。

### Literal Translation

If [the disease] arises from injury by food, drink, and toil, its external evidence must show at the mouth: one must lose the flavor of grain, the abdomen must be disharmonious, and one must be unwilling to speak. Even if against one's will one makes forced reply, the voice must be timid and weak; there is foaming saliva at the mouth and profuse spittle; clear nasal mucus is sometimes present, sometimes absent — this is the yin pattern. In external contraction of wind-cold, the external evidence must show at the nose: the nasal qi is not free, the voice is heavy, turbid, and not clear, its speech is blocked and stuffy, [yet] vigorous and forceful, and the mouth must be harmonious within. In cold damage, the face is red and the nose is stuffed and dry; in wind damage, at most the nose flows clear mucus [and nothing more]. The Suwen states: "The nose is the visitation of the lung;" the lung qi communicates with Heaven, so in external contraction of wind-cold the nose becomes not free. The mouth is the kuntu [earth]; the spleen qi communicates with the mouth, so in injury by loss of regulation in food and drink and toil, the mouth does not know the flavor of grain, nor does it know the five flavors. It is also said: "[In] injury from food, aversion to food [arises]"— that harm from food is plainly shown.
