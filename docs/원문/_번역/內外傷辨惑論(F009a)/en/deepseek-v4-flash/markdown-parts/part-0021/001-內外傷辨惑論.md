## 飲食自倍腸胃乃傷分而治之。

### Source Text

痹論云：陰氣者，靜則神藏，躁則消亡，飲食自倍，腸胃乃傷，此混言之也，分之為二，飲也，食也，又經云：因而大飲則氣逆，因而飽食筋脈橫解則腸澼為痔，飲者，無形之氣，傷之則宜發汗利小便，使上下分消其濕，解醒湯五苓散之類主之，食者，有形之物，傷之則宜損其穀，其次莫若消導，丁香爛飯丸枳朮丸之類主之，稍重則攻化，三稜消積丸木香見睍丸之類主之，尤重者則或吐或下，瓜蒂散備急丸之類主之，以平為期，蓋脾已傷，又以藥傷使營運之氣減削，食愈難消，故五常政論云：大毒治病，十去其六，常毒治病，十去其七，小毒治病，十去其八，無毒治病，十去其九，穀肉果菜，食養盡之，無使過之，傷其正也，不盡行復，如法聖人，垂此嚴戒，是為萬世福也，如能慎言語，節飲食，所謂治未病也。

### Literal Translation

The Bi Lun states: "The yin qi — in stillness the spirit is stored; in agitation it is extinguished; when food and drink are doubled by oneself, the intestines and stomach are thereby injured." This is speaking comprehensively of it. Dividing it into two: drink and food. And the Classic states: "Thus, in great drinking, qi then rebels; thus, in satiety eating, the sinews and vessels become transverse and slack, then intestinal prolapse becomes hemorrhoids." Drink is the qi without form; when [it is] injured, one should induce sweating and promote urination, so that above and below separately disperse its dampness — [the medicaments] such as [Ge Hua] Jie Xing Tang and Wu Ling San govern. Food is a thing with form; when [it is] injured, one should deplete its grain, and next best nothing surpasses dispersing and guiding — [the medicaments] such as Ding Xiang Lan Fan Wan and Zhi Zhu Wan govern. Somewhat severe, then attack and transform — [the medicaments] such as San Leng Xiao Ji Wan and Mu Xiang Jian Xian Wan govern. The most severe, then either vomit or drain — [the medicaments] such as Gua Di San and Bei Ji Wan govern. Taking leveling as the criterion. Because the spleen is already injured and moreover, with the medicament injuring [it], the circulating transporting qi is reduced and pared, and the food becomes all the harder to digest. Hence the Wu Chang Zheng Lun states: "In treating disease with great poison, [one] removes six of ten; treating disease with ordinary poison, [one] removes seven of ten; treating disease with minor poison, [one] removes eight of ten; treating disease with no poison, [one] removes nine of ten. With grain, flesh, fruit, and vegetables, one nourishes and exhausts it; do not let [the treatment] exceed [this], wounding the orthopathic [qi]. If it is not completely carried out, repeat it again, as in the law." The sage, holding up this stern warning, makes it a blessing for ten thousand generations. If one is able to be cautious in speech and temperate in food and drink, this is what is called 'treating the not-yet-diseased.'
