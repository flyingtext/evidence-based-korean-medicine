## 序

### Source Text

此方，乃唐會昌間，有一頭陀，結草菴於宜春之鐘村，貌甚古，年百四五十歲。買數畝墾畲種粟以自給。村氓有彭叟者，常常往來其廬，顏情甚捻，或助之耕。一日，彭之子，升木伐條，誤墜於地，折頸挫肱，呻吟不絕。彭訴於道人，道人請視之，命買數品藥，親制以餌。俄而痛定，數日已如平時。始知道人能醫，求者益眾。道人亦厭之。乃取方授彭，使自制以應求者，且誓之以無苟取，毋輕售，毋傳非人。由是言治損者宗彭氏。彭叟之初識道人三十許，今老矣，然風采無異前時。問其姓名，曰藺道者。問其氏，曰長安人也。始道人閉門不通人事，人亦少至，惟一鄧先生，每春晴秋爽，攜稚過之，必載酒餚從焉。道人懸一椰瓢壁間，鄧至則取瓢更酌，彭或遇之亦酌。二人皆談笑竟晷。醉則高歌，其詞曰：經世學，經世學成無用著；山中樂，山中樂土堪耕鑿；癭瓢有酒同君酌，醉臥草廬誰喚覺；松陰忽聽雙鳴鶴，起來日出穿林薄。彭踳樸不知所言為何，惟熟聽其歌，亦得其腔。每歸對人歌之，人亦不省。居久，鄧先生不至，彭問道人，道人云已仙去，彭卒不悟。後江西觀察使行部至袁州，聞彭所歌，異之，詰其詞，得道人姓氏，遂遣人同彭叟至其廬邀之。至則行矣，惟瓢存焉。廉大以為恨，謂彭傳其治損諸方，因易其村曰鞏，道人有書數篇，所授者特其最後一卷云。

### Literal Translation

This formula comes from the Huichang era of the Tang dynasty. There was a mendicant monk (touduo) who built a thatched hut in Zhong village of Yichun. His appearance was very ancient, and he was one hundred forty or fifty years old. He bought several mu of land, cleared it for slash-and-burn farming, and planted millet to support himself. Among the villagers there was an old man surnamed Peng who often came and went to his hut; their feelings were very close, and sometimes he helped him with the plowing. One day, Peng's son climbed a tree to cut branches, mistakenly fell to the ground, broke his neck and injured his arm, and moaned without stopping. Peng told the Daoist (renunciant) about it. The Daoist asked to look at him, ordered several kinds of medicine to be bought, and personally prepared them to feed him. In a short while the pain settled, and in a few days he was already as before. Only then did people begin to know that the Daoist could practice medicine, and those who sought him became more numerous. The Daoist also grew weary of this. He then took out the formulas and gave them to Peng, telling him to prepare them himself to respond to those who sought them, and moreover swore him not to take them for nothing, not to sell them lightly, and not to transmit them to unsuitable people. From this, those who spoke of treating injuries all honored the Peng family. When old man Peng first knew the Daoist he was about thirty years old; now he is old, yet his bearing is no different from before. When asked his name, he said he was the Daoist Lin. When asked his surname, he said he was a man of Chang'an. At first the Daoist closed his door and did not engage in worldly affairs, and few people came to him. There was only one Mr. Deng who, every spring when the weather was clear and autumn when it was cool, would bring his young child to visit him, and would always bring wine and food along. The Daoist hung a coconut ladle on the wall; when Deng came, he would take the ladle and pour more wine, and if Peng happened to meet him there he would also drink. The two of them would both laugh and talk for the whole day. When drunk they would sing loudly, and the words were: 'The learning of governing the world, the learning of governing the world, when completed, is of no use; the joy of the mountains, the joy of the mountains, the land is fit for plowing and digging; the gourd ladle has wine, I drink with you, drunk and lying in the thatched hut, who calls me awake; in the shade of the pines I suddenly hear a pair of cranes cry, I rise and the sun comes out, passing through the sparse grove.' Peng was simple and dull and did not know what the words meant; he only listened carefully to the song and also caught its tune. Whenever he returned he would sing it to people, but people also did not understand it. After a long time, Mr. Deng did not come. Peng asked the Daoist about him, and the Daoist said he had already gone off to become an immortal. Peng ultimately did not understand. Later, when the Jiangxi guanchashi (surveillance commissioner) traveled through his circuit and reached Yuanzhou, he heard Peng's song, found it strange, and questioned him about the words. He obtained the Daoist's name and surname, and then sent a man together with old man Peng to his hut to invite him. When they arrived, he had already left; only the ladle remained. Lian (the commissioner) greatly regretted this, and told Peng to transmit his various formulas for treating injuries. He therefore renamed the village Gong. The Daoist had several books of formulas; what he transmitted was only the last volume of them.
