### Literal Translation

Treats injuries from falls and blows, broken bones and severed sinews, pain and swelling. Blood stasis in the abdomen, pouring into the four limbs, restlessness and distress. Carbuncles and deep abscesses on the back, rotten flesh and sinews. All kinds of wind disorders, left-sided paralysis and right-sided paralysis, numbness of the hands and feet, and women's blood-wind attacks. All are suitable to take. Each time take half a pill, grind and dissolve it in ash-free wine, slightly decoct for three to five boils, and take warm. Not limited to a fixed time, and not limited in amount. This medicine should always be kept in a gauze or kudzu-cloth bag hung in a clean place; it will not spoil for a long time and can be kept for emergencies. Only pregnant women must not take it.

Jingjie/Schizonepeta two and a half liang, Fengxiang/Liquidambar resin one liang (ground separately), Tanxiang/sandalwood one liang (not exposed to fire), Jiangzhenjie/Dalbergia heartwood one liang, Caowu/Aconiti kusnezoffii two liang (wine-boiled), Shangui/Cinnamon (remove the coarse bark), Danggui/Angelica (soaked in wine one shichen), Cangzhu/Atractylodes (soaked in rice water, five days in spring, three in summer, seven in autumn, ten in winter, stir-fried dry), Chuan Qianghuo/Notopterygium (remove the root), Baiji/Bletilla (wrapped in dough and roasted in embers, sun-dried), Wudou/black bean (stir-fried with glutinous rice until yellow), Dilong/earthworm (remove the earth), each half a liang, Diqing one and a half qian (ground separately), Shexiang/musk half a liang (ground separately), Chuanxiong/Ligusticum half a liang (washed in hot water three times), Wulingzhi/Trogopterus dung one and a half liang (ground separately with lampwick), Ruxiang/Frankincense one liang (ground separately), Moyao/Myrrh one liang (ground separately), Chuanwu/Aconiti radix two liang (paozi/parched), Gusuibu/Drynaria (remove the fuzz, stir-fry), Chuan Niuxi/Achyranthes (soaked in wine one shichen), Xixin/Asarum (remove the sprouts), Huasangmu/flowering mulberry wood (burned to preserve its nature), Baizhi/Angelica dahurica (unworm-eaten ones), Chishaoyao/red peony (soaked in wine), Chuan Qianniu/Pharbitis (stir-fried with lime), Nanxing/Arisaema (stir-fried with lime until yellow), Zirantong/Pyritum (calcined, quenched in wine, ground separately), Dali jian/chestnut, each half a liang, Mubie/twenty pieces (remove the oil and shell). The above are ground into a fine powder, and wine is used to cook a flour paste to form pills the size of a pellet (danzi). Put into a mortar and pound thirty-odd pestles, form into a lump, weigh one liang, and divide into three pills. When all the pills are finished, divide into three parts: one part dried in the shade, one part sun-dried for half a shichen, one part dried/baked for half a shichen. Then mix the three parts together in one place, so that yin and yang combine, until the medicine gives off a dusty qi. Then brush off the dust, and use black lacquer gloss as the coating.
