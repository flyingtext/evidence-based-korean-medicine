## 小引

### Source Text

聖《經》云：壹是皆以修身為本，其本亂而末治者否矣。垂訓簡易，萬世師法也。稽諸所以修者，則曰正心誠意，又曰齊明盛服，非禮不動，此修於無病時也。

### Literal Translation

The Sage's 《Classic》 says: All things take cultivating the self as the root; if the root is disordered, then the branches cannot be well governed. The instruction handed down is simple and easy, and is the model for ten thousand generations. Examining the means of cultivation, it is said to rectify the mind and make the will sincere, and also to fast and purify and wear solemn garments, and not to move without propriety; this is cultivation during times of no illness.

## 小引

### Source Text

《易》曰：懲忿窒欲，又曰：慎言語、節飲食。此修於未病前也。若病臨於身，只以醫藥為修矣，《靈樞》、《素問》，歷歷可考。

### Literal Translation

The 《Changes》 says: Repress anger and stop desire, and also says: Be cautious in speech and moderate in food and drink. This is cultivation before illness arises. If illness comes upon the body, then one only uses medicine as cultivation; the 《Lingshu》 and 《Suwen》 can be examined in detail.

### Source Text

然亦有治病去其八九之論也，推及其至淺則可治，深則不治。病至於不治，則束手待盡者有之，欲罷不能者亦有之，豈非人生恨事乎？設有良醫既能治病，且能去病之根，使受病者目見為快，即死已無憾矣。況翻然可不死乎？況從此可長生乎？何幸如之！何樂如之！不死若何？去其病也；長生若何？去病根也；去根若何？去其積也；去積若何？有一言而可以終身行之者，曰「揉」。

### Literal Translation

Yet there is also the theory of treating disease and removing eight or nine parts of it. Extending this, if it is extremely shallow it can be treated, but if deep it cannot be treated. When the disease reaches the point of being untreatable, there are those who fold their hands and wait for the end, and there are also those who want to stop but cannot; is this not a regret of life? Suppose there is a good physician who can both treat the disease and remove the root of the disease, so that the one suffering sees it and is pleased; even if he dies, he has no regret. How much more can he, turning around, not die? How much more can he from this attain longevity? What fortune is like this! What joy is like this! How not to die? Remove the disease. How to attain longevity? Remove the root of the disease. How to remove the root? Remove the accumulation (積). How to remove the accumulation? There is one word that can be practiced for a lifetime, and it is called “rubbing” (揉).

### Source Text

其揉若何？請假賢傳引《詩》之語為則，曰如切如磋，如琢如磨者，自修也。若是易知易能，人人得為，何待於言？正恐其道在邇，而求諸遠；事在易而求諸難也。故縷述經驗，信而有證，為修身者忠告而善道之。

### Literal Translation

What is this rubbing? Let me borrow the words that the worthy commentary quotes from the 《Poetry》 as the standard: like cutting and filing, like chiseling and grinding, this is self-cultivation. If it is this easy to know and easy to do, and everyone can do it, why wait for words? It is precisely because I fear that the Way is near, yet one seeks it in the far; the matter is easy, yet one seeks it in the difficult. Therefore I set forth my experience in detail, trustworthy and with evidence, as a loyal admonition and good guidance for those who cultivate the self.

### Source Text

《素問》有云：無形無患。是有形者皆有患也，能不思所以然設法去之乎？《詩》云：周雖舊邦，其命維新。是故君子無所不用其極。

### Literal Translation

The 《Suwen》 says: If there is no form, there is no affliction. This means that all that has form has affliction; can one not think of the reason and devise a method to remove it? The 《Poetry》 says: Though Zhou is an old state, its mandate is renewed. Therefore the gentleman uses every means to the utmost.
