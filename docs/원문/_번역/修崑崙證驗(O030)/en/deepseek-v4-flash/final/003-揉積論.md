## 揉積論

### Source Text

夫微之顯者，積也。人身皮里膜內必有津液滋潤其間，乃氣血之所生也。及氣血因感傷而停滯，則津液變涎沫以凝結，氣血可以復通，凝結不能再解，潛孳暗長，無減有增，此積之所由成也。

### Literal Translation

Now, what is hidden yet becomes manifest is the accumulation (積). Within the skin and membranes of the human body there must be fluids (津液) moistening the spaces between, for these are what qi and blood generate. When qi and blood stagnate because of injury from external influences, then the fluids change into saliva and froth and congeal. Qi and blood can be restored to flow, but the congealed matter cannot be dissolved again; it secretly breeds and grows in darkness, never decreasing but only increasing. This is how accumulation comes to be formed.

## 揉積論

### Source Text

若銅鐵遇潮濕生鏽，非括磨不能去，正如積之非揉不消，同一理也。人生幼稚無積，積生則絕。少年氣血旺，積不能生，壯年氣血更旺，嗜欲開，難免積，隨長隨消。

### Literal Translation

Just as copper and iron, meeting dampness, produce rust that cannot be removed except by scraping and grinding, so too accumulation cannot be dispersed except by rubbing; it is the same principle. In human life, in childhood there is no accumulation; when accumulation arises, then life is cut off. In youth qi and blood are vigorous, and accumulation cannot arise. In the prime of life qi and blood are even more vigorous, but desires open up, and accumulation is hard to avoid, growing and dispersing along the way.

Someone says: What you speak of are all internal symptoms of disease; in ancient times there were medical cases and formulas. Where do you see this so-called accumulation? Where do you see that rubbing is effective? Since ancient times there has been no such method of treating disease.

## 揉積論

### Source Text

予應之曰：子遵古而言內症是也，獨不見生於外之癭瘤乎？附體成形，耗其氣血，日長月大，竟以致命。雖有筋脂膿血石肉之別，要皆氣血凝結之積，古方皆以藥內消，然歷見消去者，十不獲一。緣病在皮里膜內，藥力不能到也，在外揉之，竟可消散。

### Literal Translation

I answer him, saying: You follow the ancients and speak of internal symptoms, which is correct. But do you not see the goiters and tumors (癭瘤) that arise externally? They attach to the body and take form, consuming its qi and blood, growing day by day and month by month, and finally causing death. Although there are distinctions of sinew, fat, pus, blood, stone, and flesh, in essence they are all accumulations of congealed qi and blood. The ancient formulas all use medicine to disperse them internally, yet in all I have seen, not one in ten is dispersed. This is because the disease is within the skin and membranes, and the power of medicine cannot reach it. Rubbing it externally can actually disperse it.

## 揉積論

### Source Text

今之所謂積者，即如癭瘤使之反生於內，得不統謂氣血之積乎？獨可專仗藥力消之乎？總之，凡百病症，皆以氣血為主，通則無積，不通則積，新則積小，久則積大。不論大小內外病症，果能揉之，使經絡氣血通暢，則病無不愈者，不必先爭此揉積之名分今古也。再以淺近者申之，如頭痛揉提太陽及眉心，立見輕爽，喉痛重提項前，亦見效驗；小有腫痛瘡癤，揉之立時解散。

### Literal Translation

The so-called accumulation of today is just like making the goiter and tumor grow internally instead; can one not call it all an accumulation of qi and blood? Can one rely solely on the power of medicine to disperse it? In sum, all the hundred diseases take qi and blood as primary. If it flows, there is no accumulation; if it does not flow, there is accumulation. If new, the accumulation is small; if old, the accumulation is large. Regardless of large or small, internal or external disease, if one can truly rub it so that the channels and collaterals and qi and blood flow freely, then there is no disease that will not be cured. There is no need to first dispute the name and the ancient-modern distinction of this rubbing of accumulation. Let me further explain with something shallow and near: for headache, rub and lift the temple (太陽) and the space between the brows (眉心), and one immediately feels light and refreshed; for sore throat, lift the front of the neck heavily, and one also sees effect; for a small swelling, pain, sore, or boil, rubbing it disperses it at once.

## 揉積論

### Source Text

揉之為法，有益無損，且可窒病之源，拔病之根，思患預防之道，無過是者，豈反不及臨渴掘井之醫藥耶？且也，病遇良醫實是罕逢，而遠鄉僻野，更無所謂醫藥矣。況疳勞鼓嗝，醫效難期：肓上膏下，藥力不到，更有無力延醫市藥者，尤堪憐憫也。俗語云有病靠天，此無法如何之詞也。

### Literal Translation

As a method, rubbing has benefit and no harm, and moreover it can block the source of disease and pull out the root of disease. In the way of thinking of harm and preventing it, nothing surpasses this. How could it be inferior to the medicine of digging a well when one is thirsty? Moreover, meeting a good physician is truly rare, and in distant villages and remote wilds there is even less of what is called medicine. How much more for consumption, fatigue, drum distension, and hiccups, where medical effect is hard to expect; above the diaphragm and below the fat, the power of medicine cannot reach. There are even those who lack the strength to summon a physician and buy medicine, who are especially to be pitied. The common saying goes, “If one has illness, rely on Heaven,” which is the speech of one who has no method and cannot do anything about it.

## 揉積論

### Source Text

從古良醫代不乏人，真可起死回生，實是出神入化，然垂名千古而心法無傳，徒留醫案方藥，後人則效無從。今茲之道，若大路然，人人能為，時時可行，惟患人之不求，不患法之不傳也。集說既成，弁以揉積論，願先覽焉。

### Literal Translation

From ancient times good physicians have not been lacking in each generation; truly they could raise the dead and restore life, truly entering the spirit and leaving the transformation. Yet they left their names for a thousand ages while their heart-methods were not transmitted, leaving only medical cases and formulas, so that later people had nothing to follow. The present Way is like a great road: everyone can do it, and at any time it can be practiced. One only worries that people do not seek it, not that the method is not transmitted. The collected sayings being complete, I place the Discourse on Rubbing Accumulation at the head, and wish that it be read first.
