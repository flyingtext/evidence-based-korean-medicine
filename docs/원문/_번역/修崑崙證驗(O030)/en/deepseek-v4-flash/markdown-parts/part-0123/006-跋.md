## 跋

### Source Text

心之積與火之灰同，心之思不思，系積之有無輕重焉。有說乎？曰有，請以中風病證之，卒然不省，尚能飲食，非積之蔽心重乎？言語模糊，猶可半了，非積之蔽心輕乎？次則明少暗多，繼則明多暗少，以漸輕以漸明，明則能思矣，仍以多少分耳。推而言之，人之殘忍、，刻薄、乖戾、執拗，孰無人心，而若是之異乎？必是由積蔽所致，又分輕重矣。

### Literal Translation

The accumulation of the heart is the same as the ashes of the fire; whether the heart thinks or does not think depends on the presence and severity of the accumulation. Is there an explanation? I say there is; let me prove it with the disease of wind-stroke. Suddenly unconscious, yet still able to eat and drink; is this not the accumulation obscuring the heart heavily? Speech is blurred, yet one can still half understand; is this not the accumulation obscuring the heart lightly? Next, the bright is little and the dark is much; then the bright is much and the dark is little, gradually lighter and gradually brighter; when bright, one can think, still distinguished by how much and how little. Extending this, in people's cruelty, stinginess, perversity, and obstinacy, who lacks a human heart, yet they are so different? It must be caused by the obscuring of accumulation, and again it is divided into light and heavy.
