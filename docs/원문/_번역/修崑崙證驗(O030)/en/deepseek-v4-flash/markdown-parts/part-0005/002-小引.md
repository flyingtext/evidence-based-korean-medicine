## 小引

### Source Text

然亦有治病去其八九之論也，推及其至淺則可治，深則不治。病至於不治，則束手待盡者有之，欲罷不能者亦有之，豈非人生恨事乎？設有良醫既能治病，且能去病之根，使受病者目見為快，即死已無憾矣。況翻然可不死乎？況從此可長生乎？何幸如之！何樂如之！不死若何？去其病也；長生若何？去病根也；去根若何？去其積也；去積若何？有一言而可以終身行之者，曰「揉」。

### Literal Translation

Yet there is also the theory of treating disease and removing eight or nine parts of it. Extending this, if it is extremely shallow it can be treated, but if deep it cannot be treated. When the disease reaches the point of being untreatable, there are those who fold their hands and wait for the end, and there are also those who want to stop but cannot; is this not a regret of life? Suppose there is a good physician who can both treat the disease and remove the root of the disease, so that the one suffering sees it and is pleased; even if he dies, he has no regret. How much more can he, turning around, not die? How much more can he from this attain longevity? What fortune is like this! What joy is like this! How not to die? Remove the disease. How to attain longevity? Remove the root of the disease. How to remove the root? Remove the accumulation (積). How to remove the accumulation? There is one word that can be practiced for a lifetime, and it is called “rubbing” (揉).
