## 自序

### Source Text

嘗讀仲景書。止言舌白、苔滑。並無黃、黑、刺、裂。至金鏡錄始集三十六圖。逮後觀舌心法。廣至一百三十有七。

何後世證變之多若此。寧知傷寒自表傳裡。舌苔必由白滑而變他色。不似伏邪瘟疫等熱毒。自內達外之一病便見黃黑諸苔也。觀仲景論中。一見舌白、苔滑。即言難治。安有失治而致變者乎。所以仲景止言白苔。已見一斑。不煩瑣屑。後人無先聖治未病之能。勢不得不反復辨論以啟蒙昧。蓋邪氣入裡。其虛實寒熱之機。必現於舌。非若脈法之隱而不顯也。

況陰盛格陽。與邪熱鬱伏。多有假證假脈。惟驗舌上苔色之滑、燥、厚、薄。昭若冰鑒。無所遁形。由是取觀舌心法。

正其錯誤。削其繁蕪。汰其無預於傷寒者。而參入家大人治案所紀。及己所親歷。共得一百二十圖。命曰傷寒舌鑒。授之剞劂。以公同志臨證之一助云。

<ins>康熙戊申年秋月誕先張登書於　永堂</ins>

### Literal Translation

I once read the book of Zhongjing. It only speaks of a white tongue and a slippery coating, and has no [mention of] yellow, black, thorns, or fissures. Only with the Jinjing lu (Golden Mirror Record) did it begin to collect thirty-six diagrams. Later, when I came to the Guanshe xinfa (Heart Method of Observing the Tongue), it was expanded to one hundred and thirty-seven.

How is it that the transformations of patterns in later generations are so numerous as this? How could one not know that in cold damage, [the pathogen] transmits from the exterior to the interior, and that the tongue coating must necessarily change from white and slippery to other colors, unlike the heat toxins of latent evil and pestilence, which reach outward from within and show yellow and black coatings at the very onset of the illness. In the treatises of Zhongjing, as soon as a white tongue and slippery coating are seen, it is immediately said to be difficult to treat. How could there be cases that change because of failure to treat? Therefore Zhongjing only speaks of the white coating, already showing one aspect, and does not trouble with trifling details. Later people do not have the ability of the former sages to treat before illness arises, so it is unavoidable that they must repeatedly discuss and discriminate in order to enlighten the ignorant. For when evil qi enters the interior, the mechanisms of its vacuity, repletion, cold, and heat must appear on the tongue, unlike the pulse method, which is hidden and not manifest.

Moreover, in yin exuberance repelling yang, and in the depressed and hidden heat evil, there are often false patterns and false pulses. Only by examining the slippery, dry, thick, and thin of the coating color on the tongue, [the truth] is as clear as a mirror, and there is no place for it to hide its form. From this I took the Guanshe xinfa.

I corrected its errors, pruned its redundant and overgrown [parts], discarded those that had no bearing on cold damage, and incorporated what was recorded in my father's case records and what I myself had personally experienced, obtaining in all one hundred and twenty diagrams. I named it Shanghan shejian (Cold Damage Tongue Mirror). I delivered it to the block carvers, to share it with colleagues as an aid in clinical examination.

<ins>Written in the autumn month of the wushen year of the Kangxi reign by Zhang Deng, born [in the year of] Dan, at the Yong Hall</ins>
