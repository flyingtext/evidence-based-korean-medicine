夫手法者，謂以兩手安置所傷之筋骨，使仍復於舊也。但傷有重輕，而手法各有所宜。其痊可之遲速，及遺留殘疾與否，皆關乎手法之所施得宜，或失其宜，或未盡其法也。

Now, the manual techniques (手法) mean using both hands to set the injured sinews and bones (筋骨) so that they return to their former state. Whether recovery is fast or slow, and whether residual disability remains or not, all depend on whether the technique is applied appropriately, or loses its appropriateness, or does not fully carry out its method.

### Literal Translation

The bony frame of the whole body is not uniform, and the arrangement, order, and affiliation of the twelve sinew channels (十二經筋) also differ in each case. Therefore one must always know the bodily form, and recognize the regions. Once one faces a case, the mechanism touches from without, skill is born within, the hand follows the mind in turning, and the method issues from the hand. Sometimes one pulls it apart and then rejoins it, sometimes one pushes it into place and restores its position, sometimes one corrects its obliquity, sometimes one completes its deficiency. Then whether the bone is cut off, shattered, or obliquely broken, and whether the sinew is slack, relaxed, coiled, contracted, turned over, rotated, separated, or joined—although these are within the flesh, by palpating with the hand one naturally comprehends the condition. When the method is applied so that the patient does not feel its pain, only then is it called a manual technique. Moreover, the injured places often concern life itself. For example, the seven orifices (七竅) open upward to the brain and marrow, and the diaphragm is near the heart, the sovereign. When the four extremities are injured and the pain enters the heart, even if the person's primordial qi (元氣) is ordinarily strong and the stagnant blood easily disperses, so that recovery can be expected within a fixed period, the technique still must not be applied recklessly. If the primordial qi is ordinarily weak and, once injured, the condition is already hard to sustain, then if the technique is again mistaken, it will be extremely difficult to retrieve. This is why one must be especially cautious and careful.
