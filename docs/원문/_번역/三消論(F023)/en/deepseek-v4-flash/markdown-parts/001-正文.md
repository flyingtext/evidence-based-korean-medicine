## 正文

### Source Text

《易》言：天地自太虛至黃泉，有六位，《內經》言：人之身，自頭至足，亦有六位，今余又言：人胸腹之間，自肺至腎，又有六位，人與天地，造化五行，同一爐備，知彼則知此矣。故立天之氣，曰金與火，立地之氣，曰土與水，立人之氣，曰風與火，故金火合則熱而清，水土合則濕而寒，風火合則溫而炎。

人胸腹之間，亦猶是也。

### Literal Translation

The Book of Changes says: from the Great Void (太虛) to the Yellow Springs (黃泉), heaven and earth have six positions. The Inner Canon (內經) says: the human body, from head to foot, also has six positions. Now I further say: within the human chest and abdomen, from the Lungs to the Kidneys, there are also six positions. Humans and heaven and earth, in the transformation and creation of the Five Phases, are cast in the same furnace; knowing the one, one knows the other. Therefore, the qi that establishes heaven is called Metal and Fire; the qi that establishes earth is called Earth and Water; the qi that establishes humans is called Wind and Fire. Thus when Metal and Fire combine, it is hot and clear; when Earth and Water combine, it is damp and cold; when Wind and Fire combine, it is warm and scorching.

Within the human chest and abdomen, it is likewise so.

### Translator's Notes

- 太虛/黃泉 rendered as Great Void/Yellow Springs with characters.
- 五行 kept as Five Phases (standard English Chinese medicine term).

### Literal Translation

The Great Treatise on the Regular Government of the Five [Phases] (五常政大論) says: that which is rooted in the center is called the spirit mechanism (神機); these are the animals, whose root and foundation lie in the center. The root and foundation are the Spleen, Stomach, and Kidneys. (This explanation of the classic is forced; the meaning of the classic is not like this. The classic text has two meanings: one speaks of the frame and members of animals, which obey the center, hence "rooted in the center"; the branches and leaves of plants grow forth below, hence "rooted in the exterior." The other speaks of the spirit, intelligence, thought, and deliberation of humans arising in the center, hence "rooted in the center," while the nutritive and defensive qi and blood circulate in the exterior, hence "rooted in the exterior." If the center is destroyed, there is no awareness; if the exterior ceases, there is no movement. It is not a matter of the Spleen and Kidneys. What the later Original Patterns of Disease (原病式) discusses is extremely correct.) When food enters the stomach, the Spleen distributes and transforms its qi and flavor, nourishing the five viscera and the hundred bones. Thus sour enters the Liver and nourishes the sinews and membranes; bitter enters the Heart and nourishes the blood and vessels; sweet enters the Spleen and nourishes the flesh and muscles; pungent enters the Lungs and nourishes the skin and body hair; salty enters the Kidneys and nourishes the bone marrow.
