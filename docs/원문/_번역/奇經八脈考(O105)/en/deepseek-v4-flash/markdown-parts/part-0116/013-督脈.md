## O105:p013:t0007

### Source Text

滑伯仁曰:任、督二脈, 一源而二岐, 一行於身之前, 一行於身之後, 人身之有任、督, 猶天地之有子、午, 可以分可以合, 分之以見陰陽之不離, 合之以見渾淪之無間, 一而二二而一者也. 李瀕湖曰:任、督二脈, 人身之子、午也. 乃丹家陽火陰符升降之道, 坎水離火交媾之鄉.

### Literal Translation

Hua Boren said: The Ren and Du two vessels have one source but two branches; one runs in front of the body and one runs behind the body. The presence of Ren and Du in the human body is like the presence of zi (north) and wu (south) in heaven and earth; they can be separated and can be united. Separating them shows that yin and yang do not depart; uniting them shows that the undifferentiated whole has no gap; they are one yet two, and two yet one. Li Binhu said: The Ren and Du two vessels are the zi and wu of the human body. They are the pathway of the ascent and descent of the yang fire and yin talisman of the alchemists, and the place of intercourse of kan water and li fire.
