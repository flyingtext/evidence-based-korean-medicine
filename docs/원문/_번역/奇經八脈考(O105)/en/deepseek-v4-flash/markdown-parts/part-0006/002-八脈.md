## O105:p002:t0002

### Source Text

督脈起於會陰, 循背而行於身之後, 為陽脈之總督, 故曰陽脈之海;任脈起於會陰, 循腹而行於身之前, 為陰脈之承任, 故曰陰脈之海;衝脈起於會陰, 夾臍而行, 直衝於上, 為諸脈之衝要, 故曰十二經脈之海, 帶脈則橫圍於腰, 狀如束帶, 所以總約諸脈者也. 是故陽維主一身之表, 陰維主一身之裡, 以乾坤言也. 陽蹺主一身左右之陽, 陰蹺主一身左右之陰, 以東西言也.

### Literal Translation

The Du meridian arises at Huiyin, follows the back, and runs behind the body; being the chief governor of the yang vessels, it is therefore called the Sea of the Yang Vessels. The Ren meridian arises at Huiyin, follows the abdomen, and runs in front of the body; being that which undertakes and bears (chengren) the yin vessels, it is therefore called the Sea of the Yin Vessels. The Chong meridian arises at Huiyin, flanks the navel as it runs, and rushes straight upward; being the crucial thoroughfare of the various vessels, it is therefore called the Sea of the Twelve Meridians. The Dai meridian, on the other hand, encircles the waist transversely with a form like a girdle, and so it is the one that binds together all the vessels. For this reason the Yang Wei governs the entire exterior of the body, and the Yin Wei governs the entire interior of the body — this is spoken of in terms of Qian and Kun (heaven and earth). The Yang Qiao governs the yang of the left and right of the body, and the Yin Qiao governs the yin of the left and right of the body — this is spoken of in terms of east and west.
