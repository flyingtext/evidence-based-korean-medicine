## 重刻《外台秘要方》序

### Source Text

程子敬通，醇儒也，而旁通於醫。吾於醫固未之學，而一接程子指脈說病則目無全人，微言高論疊見層生，聞未聞，解未解。程子之於醫幾乎﹗道又見其醫人也，雖極賤貧，但一接手則必端問詳審，反復精思，未嘗有厭怠之色。

### Literal Translation

Master Cheng Jingtong is a pure Confucian scholar, and he is also versed in medicine. I have never studied medicine myself, but once I encountered Master Cheng taking the pulse and explaining illness, there was no whole person in my eyes; subtle words and lofty discourses appeared in layers, hearing what I had not heard and understanding what I had not understood. How nearly Master Cheng approaches medicine! I also saw him treating people: even for the extremely poor and lowly, once he took up a case he would always ask carefully and examine thoroughly, reflect and think precisely, and never show a look of weariness or sloth.

### Source Text

其疑難者，多至盈時，惟恐少誤，無惑而後動其心。行復如是，是其人一第而為宰官，推是道也，用是心也，以往其謀國，以善萬裡生民之寄，豈可及哉？有唐王刺史《外台秘要》一書，更五代，至宋孫中丞一較正行之，及今而絕。程子不忍圖令其流布人間而為余言。

### Literal Translation

For the doubtful and difficult cases, he would often spend a full hour, only fearing a slight error, and only after there was no doubt would he move his mind. His conduct was again like this. If this man, having once passed the examination and become a governing official, extended this Way and used this mind to go and plan for his state and to fulfill well the trust of the people of ten thousand li, how could one match him? There is the book 《Waitai Miyao》 (Arcane Essentials from the Imperial Library) by Prefect Wang of the Tang dynasty. It passed through the Five Dynasties, and in the Song dynasty Censor-in-Chief Sun once corrected and published it, and it has been cut off down to the present. Master Cheng could not bear to let it fail to circulate among people, and so he spoke to me of it.

### Literal Translation

I then asked Master Cheng: If one wishes to know medicine, by what way can one become proficient? He said: One must love learning. What shall one study? He said: First read books. Can reading books make one a physician? He said: There are those who read books and yet cannot be physicians, but there is certainly no one who can be a physician without reading books.

## 重刻《外台秘要方》序

### Source Text

嗚呼﹗天下事皆然耳，獨醫也乎？余因問程子：以子之道，醫世之人，當吾世而既日不暇給，及身後而書不盡言，言不盡意，吾懼其不傳也。盍得其人而傳之，何以書？程子曰：師之求弟，甚於弟之求師，有來學人，吾愿盡所學焉，爾而無如今之來學人，未嘗求學，徒以求行也。是道也，非苦心十年不可得。

### Literal Translation

Alas! All the affairs of the world are like this; is it only medicine? I then asked Master Cheng: With your Way, you treat the people of the world. In our own generation you are already too busy to spare time, and after your death the book will not exhaust the words, and the words will not exhaust the meaning; I fear it will not be transmitted. Why not obtain a person and transmit it to him, rather than a book? Master Cheng said: A teacher seeking a disciple is more eager than a disciple seeking a teacher. If there is a person who comes to learn, I wish to exhaust what I have learned. But unlike the learners of today, who have never sought learning but only seek to practice, this Way cannot be obtained without ten years of bitter effort.

## 重刻《外台秘要方》序

### Source Text

而今之學人，來輒問曰：欲就師，授十日，足乎？一若是其亟也，吾雖欲傳，其何從而傳？嗚呼﹗天下之學，大抵然耳，獨醫也乎？古之君子其於道也，其始也，急求人而已，及其求人不可得，則不得不有事於書，以冀其長存天地之間，其於書也，不必自著書，而章隱繼絕圖存古人之書。古人之書，其賴有心之後人以獲行世者，多矣。初不必後人之盡有心、盡好學而能讀也，而其書則不忍以不存，以為萬有一人焉，有心好學，而一能讀是書也，則是一書為遂不虛存也，更百世而當有人焉，出而視今日之敬通程子，亦猶程子之視唐宋王刺史、孫中丞兩先生也，則今日之及程子之門而欲以十日盡學其所學人，程子其亦可以謝之而無所負矣。

### Literal Translation

Yet the learners of today, when they come, immediately ask: If I go to a teacher and am taught for ten days, is that enough? If they are all this hasty, then even though I wish to transmit, from where can I transmit? Alas! The learning of the world is generally like this; is it only medicine? The gentleman of antiquity, in regard to the Way, at the beginning only urgently sought a person; and when he could not obtain a person to seek, then he had no choice but to take up the matter of books, hoping that they would long endure between Heaven and Earth. In regard to books, he did not necessarily need to write books himself, but to make manifest the hidden and continue the cut off, and to seek to preserve the books of the ancients. The books of the ancients, relying on later people of mind to obtain circulation in the world, are many. At first it is not necessary that all later people have mind and all love learning and be able to read; but the book cannot bear not to be preserved, thinking that if there is one person in ten thousand who has mind and loves learning and is able to read this book, then this one book is not preserved in vain. Even after a hundred generations there should be a person who comes forth and looks at today's Master Cheng Jingtong, just as Master Cheng looks at the two gentlemen, Prefect Wang and Censor-in-Chief Sun of the Tang and Song. Then to the person who comes to Master Cheng's gate today wishing to learn all he has learned in ten days, Master Cheng can also decline him without owing anything.
