## 33.咳嗽方法

### Source Text

肺為華蓋號清虛，忽被邪魔入心居；有聲無痰來作咳，無聲有痰嗽難除。
有聲有痰為咳嗽，令人五臟不聊驅；參蘇四季隨加減，丁香半夏作丸須。
參蘇飲內枳前胡，半夏陳皮桔梗蘆；甘草茯苓木香末，清金瀉火病皆疏。

丁香半夏丸
人參　乾薑　丁香　細辛　半夏　檳榔

脾胃溫暖，則能運行，痰飲脾胃虛寒，則痰飲停於胸膈，肺氣因之不利，乃作咳嗽。半夏之辛，所以燥脾，人參之甘所以養胃。乾薑之溫所以行痰。細辛之辛所以散飲。用檳榔取性重，可以隨痰。經云所高者抑之是也。

### Literal Translation

The lung is the canopied canopy, named clear and empty; suddenly it is invaded by an evil spirit entering its abode. Sound without phlegm comes as a dry cough (ke); no sound with phlegm, a productive cough (sou), hard to remove.
With sound and with phlegm it is cough (kesou), making one's five viscera restless without rest. Shensu (Ginseng-Perilla) follows the four seasons, adding and subtracting; Dingxiang Banxia (Clove and Pinellia) made into pills is required.
Within Shensu Yin (Ginseng-Perilla Beverage) are Zhiqiao (Bitter Orange) and Qianhu (Peucedanum); Banxia (Pinellia), Chenpi (Tangerine), Jiegeng (Platycodon) and Lu (Lugen/Reed Rhizome). Gancao (Licorice), Fuling (Poria) and Muxiang (Aucklandia) as powder; clearing metal (lung) and draining fire, all the illnesses are dredged free.

Dingxiang Banxia Wan (Clove and Pinellia Pill)
Renshen (Ginseng), Ganjiang (Dried Ginger), Dingxiang (Clove), Xixin (Asarum), Banxia (Pinellia), Binglang (Areca)

When the spleen and stomach are warm, then it can transport and move; with phlegm-rheum, the spleen and stomach being deficient and cold, then phlegm-rheum stops in the chest and diaphragm, and the lung qi thereby becomes unruly, and is then made to cough. The pungency of Banxia is that which dries the spleen; the sweet of Renshen is that which nourishes the stomach. The warmth of Ganjiang is that which moves the phlegm. The pungency of Xixin is that which disperses the rheum. Using Binglang takes its heavy nature, able to follow the phlegm. The Classics say that which is high should be pressed down — such is this.
