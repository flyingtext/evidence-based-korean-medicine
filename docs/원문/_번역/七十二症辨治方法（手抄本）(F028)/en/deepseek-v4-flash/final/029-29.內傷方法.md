## 29.內傷方法

### Source Text

內傷外感要分明，脈辨氣口與人迎；外感風寒有餘症，手背熱兮鼻氣頻。
寒熱齊作而無間，語言後重而先輕；惡風一切風都怕，惡寒向火似如冰。
內傷飲食為不足，手心壯熱苦眩頭；寒熱間作口無味，語言懶佉短聲音。
縱有些兮亦不怕，稍有溫暖即無寒；須知不足中不足，補中益氣妙如神。
補中益氣賴參耆，白朮甘草歸陳皮；柴胡左旋升麻右，內傷外感兩相宜。

中脾也，坤也，萬物之母，氣也。陽也。乾也。萬物之父。過於困乏勞碌則百骸皆虛。必盜父母以得養。而中氣大傷也，不有以補之，則形氣不幾於絕乎。故用白朮、甘草之平補者，以補中用人參、黃耆之峻補者，以益氣，土欲燥則當歸隨以潤之。氣故欲滯，則陳皮隨以利之。而升麻、柴胡所以升乎甲膽乙肝之氣也。蓋甲乙者東方生物之始，甲乙之氣升則土火木金水，必次第生也。

### Literal Translation

Interior injury and exterior contraction must be distinguished; in the pulse distinguish the mouth-qi (qikou) from the ren-ying (carotid, at the neck). Exterior contraction of wind-cold is an excess (replete) condition; the back of the hand hot and nasal breath rapid.
Cold and heat erupt together without interval; the speech heavy-laden and heavy afterwards but light at first. Aversion to wind — to all wind it is feared; aversion to cold — facing the fire yet like ice.
Interior injury from eating and drinking is a deficiency; the palms of the hands blazingly hot, bitterly dizzy-headed. Cold and heat alternating, the mouth without flavor; the speech languid, lisping, the voice short.
Though there be a little (wind-cold), also one does not fear it; with the least warmth there is then no cold. One must know the deficiency within deficiency; Buzhong Yiqi (Supplement-the-Centre and Benefit-Qi) is marvelous as a spirit.
Buzhong Yiqi relies on Shen (Renshen/Ginseng) and Qi (Huangqi/Astragalus); Baizhu (Atractylodes), Gancao (Licorice), Dang Gui and Chenpi (Tangerine Peel). Chaihu (Bupleurum) turning to the left, Shengma (Cimicifuga) to the right; interior injury and exterior contraction both suit it.

The middle-spleen (zhong) is also the Kun (earth, the receptive); it is the mother of the ten-thousand things. Qi is yang, Qian (heaven, the creative); it is the father of ten-thousand things. Passing beyond exhaustion, toil and drudgery, then the hundred limbs all become deficient. It must steal from the parents to obtain nourishment, and the middle qi is greatly wounded. Without supplementing it, would not the form and qi nearly be severed? Therefore use the level supplementary Baizhu and Gancao to supplement the middle; use the vigorous supplementary Renshen and Huangqi to benefit the qi. Earth desiring dryness, then Dang Gui follows to moisten it; the qi desiring stagnation, then Chenpi follows to free it. And Shengma and Chaihu are what raise the qi of the jia-dan (gallbladder) and yi-gan (liver). For jia and yi are the beginning of living things in the east; when the qi of jia and yi ascends, then earth, fire, wood, metal and water must be born in order one after another.
