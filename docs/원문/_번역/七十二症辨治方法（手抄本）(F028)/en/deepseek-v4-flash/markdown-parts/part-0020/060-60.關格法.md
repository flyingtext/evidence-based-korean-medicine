## 60.關格法

### Source Text

關格之症多危亡，上吐水閉喪其元；寒在上兮無入裏，是為關症水難傳。
熱在下焦難出入，號為格症四無門；格則吐逆關不尿，兩枳三陳湯不妨。
兩枳三陳治關格，二陳枳實殼南星；上下肅清通表裏，才知一鼓奏功名。

### Literal Translation

The block-and-strangury (guan-ge) condition is often one of peril and death; above, vomiting water; below, closed, destroying its primordial. Cold in the upper, with nothing entering the interior — this is the block (guan) condition, water hard to transmit.
Heat in the lower jiao, hard to exit and enter — it is named the strangury (ge) condition, no door in the four openings. Ge (strangury) then vomiting reversal; guan (block) without urination; Liangzhi San-Chen Tang (Two-Zhi Three-Matured Decoction) is no hindrance.
Liangzhi San-Chen (Two-Zhi Three-Matured) treats block-and-strangury; Erchen (Two-Matured) with Zhishi (Immature Bitter Orange), Zhiqiao (Bitter Orange shell) and Nanxing (Arisaema); above and below purified and cleared, unblocking the exterior and interior — then one knows in one stroke the drumbeat of accomplished merit.

### Translator's Notes

- 關格=guān gé (block and outflow reversal).
- 兩枳三陳湯=Liangzhi San-Chen Tang (two zhi: Zhishi+Zhiqiao; three chen: Erchen constituents).
